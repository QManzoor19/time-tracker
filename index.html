A reference week

read-only — nothing here touches your data

This is one defensible shape for a week, not a prescription, and not a scoring rubric. It exists so the empty grid has something to be compared against. The parts worth copying are structural rather than numeric: a fixed sleep window, deep work in the first few waking hours and in blocks of 90 minutes, a leak budget that is deliberately non-zero, and a weekend that is shaped differently on purpose. The exact hours should be yours.

Why it is shaped like this

Deep work sits early and in 90-minute blocks. Most people hold focused attention best in the first hours after waking, and a block long enough to get past the setup cost is worth more than the same minutes scattered. Three blocks is a full day of real output; four is unusual to sustain.

The leak budget is 30 minutes, not zero. A target of zero is the fastest way to stop logging honestly — you start rounding down, and then the one number that could change your behaviour is fiction. Give the drift a slot and see whether it stays in it.

Sleep is the anchor, not the leftover. It is placed first and everything else fits around it. It is kinded rest rather than invest deliberately: it is not virtuous, it is load-bearing, and both far too little and far too much are worth seeing.

The weekend is not a failed weekday. No deep work at all, more people, a later start. If your weekend looks like a weekday with worse numbers, the targets are the problem, not the weekend.

Judge the week, never the day. Every total here is a weekly one divided by seven. A day that misses is noise.

Kinds — invest counts toward time well spent · rest is necessary and not scored · upkeep is neutral maintenance · leak is what you want to shrink; its target is a ceiling, not a goal.

Colours — the swatch opens a full colour picker; the eight built-in hues appear in it as suggestions. Those eight are chosen to stay apart from each other under colour-blindness, so prefer them where you can. A colour you pick yourself is used exactly as picked in both light and dark mode, which is worth knowing if you choose something very dark or very pale.

Order — the arrows set the order everywhere: the paint palette, the legends, and which number key selects what. The first nine get keyboard shortcuts.

Smallest unit

This is the finest slice the day can be cut into — not how much you paint at once. That is the step control above the grid on the Day view, which defaults to 30 minutes. With a 15-minute unit and a 30-minute step, the grid looks like half-hour blocks, and any one of them can be cut in half when a half-hour genuinely held two things.

Only lower this if 15 minutes is not fine enough. A 10-minute unit gives 144 blocks a day and lets you paint in 10, 20 or 30-minute steps, at the cost of a busier grid.

Changing it rewrites every day you have logged. Going finer is lossless — each block simply splits. Going coarser is not: where a larger block covers several old ones it keeps whichever category held most of it and discards the rest. Memos move to the block containing their old start; two landing in the same block are joined, not dropped.

The week heatmap always draws at half-hour granularity whatever you pick, since finer units would make it dozens of unreadable slivers per row.

Your data

Everything lives in this browser's localStorage — nothing leaves your machine. Export monthly so a cleared cache can't take it.

How to actually keep this up

Three check-ins a day, not continuous logging. Lunch, dinner, and bed. Each takes ~20 seconds: open the day, pick a category, drag over the blocks you just lived. Recall over a 4–6 hour window is reliable; recall over a whole day is not — that's the entire reason for check-ins rather than an end-of-night entry.

Log the leak honestly or don't log it at all. The one number that changes behaviour is the one you're tempted to round down. A day that reads "3.5h drift" is a useful day.

Judge on the week, never the day. Single days are noise — a bad Tuesday means nothing. Look at Insights on Sunday, change one target, and leave the rest alone.
